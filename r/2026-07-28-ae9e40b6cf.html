US agency forces hospitals to hand ER records to an AI contractor

KFF Health News (27 Jul) reports the Consumer Product Safety Commission is compelling hospitals to feed identifiable ER records to a private contractor, Konza Health, expanding beyond its historically de-identified injury system to auto-ingest millions of full ER visit records for AI analysis. Privacy advocates flag major re-identification risk.
⚡ For you: This is the template for how 'AI needs the raw records' collides with health-data law — the exact tension HIH lives under DPDP. Use it to pressure-test whether any 1mg AI feature can run on de-identified/consented data before it touches identifiable diagnostics or Rx records; assume press and regulators treat 'private vendor + identifiable health data + AI' as headline risk.

$500 fine-tuned 9B open model beats frontier LLMs on catalog review

A team used GRPO (RL) to fine-tune a 9B open model on their e-commerce catalog-QA task; it hit 87.3% of max performance vs 76.9% for the best frontier config, at $0.50 vs $34 per 1,000 listings — 68x cheaper.

'When everyone is talking, but no one is aligned'

A Mind the Product essay (27 Jul) argues constant syncs create the illusion of alignment; teams building the same integration interpreted requirements differently once execution began. The fix: written end-to-end workflows plus a pre-build alignment review before eng starts.
⚡ For you: HIH spans pharmacy and diagnostics across multiple pods — exactly where verbal alignment silently forks. Before your next cross-pod bet, write the workflow, system boundaries and behavior-level success criteria, and run a pre-build walkthrough while changes are still cheap.

3

SlopCodeBench: coding agents still can't run 'lights-off' on real work

A benchmark that discloses requirements incrementally (like real dev) shows Opus 5 at just 24% strict pass (vs 4.6's 17%); no model passed all tests, and all degraded — duplicated code, ballooning function counts — as requirements evolved.
⚡ For you: Calibrate your AI-first bets: agents accelerate build-to-learn prototypes but need human steering loops and eval gates for production. Bake incremental verification and quality guardrails into any HIH feature shipped on agent-written code — don't scope headcount assuming autonomy.
