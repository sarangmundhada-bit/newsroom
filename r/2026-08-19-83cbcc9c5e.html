Top 3
1

Claude hits 22-35% protein binder success vs 10-15% industry norm

Anthropic published wet-lab results (Aug 18) where Claude Mythos Preview and Opus 4.8 autonomously ran de novo binder design against 15 targets and succeeded on 14, with per-design hit rates of 22.6-35.1% vs the typical 10-15%. Separately, Opus 5 turned raw NMR and LC-MS instrument data into calibrated results in ~20 minutes, matching lab purity to 96.4% vs 96.33%.
⚡ For you: Stop framing HIH's AI-in-health thesis around chat and triage — the frontier just moved to autonomous interpretation of raw instrument data. Ask your diagnostics lab partners what raw outputs (analyser files, spectra, images) 1mg currently throws away after reporting; that's the input class Claude now reads better than a technician, and the wedge no Indian competitor is set up to build on.

2

OpenAI pauses frontier training, spends 20% of compute on monitoring

Altman told TIME OpenAI paused RL training on its next model family (codenamed Astra) for over two weeks and is holding its largest planned frontier run, citing accumulated observations of 'various degrees of misalignment.' It also shipped network isolation and a monitor that inspects tool actions and reasoning traces, at an estimated ~20% compute overhead.
⚡ For you: Your build-vs-buy calculus just gained a schedule risk you cannot control: assume the next OpenAI capability step slips and that safety overhead eventually shows up in enterprise pricing. Write a vendor-swap clause into the HIH model layer now — abstract the model call so a Claude/Gemini/open-weight substitution is a config change, not a quarter of rework.

3

Harvey builds its own legal model on Kimi K3, ending pure-wrapper era

Harvey — an $11B legal AI business built entirely on other people's models — announced Harvey Tenet, its first in-house proprietary model, post-trained on mock disputes and case files atop its own version of Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3 (2.8T params, 1M context).
⚡ For you: The clearest template yet for your build-vs-buy answer: don't train from scratch, post-train an open frontier model on proprietary workflow data. HIH's equivalent asset is 1mg's Rx validation corpus, lab result histories and CCQ/PQ transcripts — scope what a post-trained open-weight model on that data would beat GPT/Claude at; that's the defensible version of the bet, not another prompt layer.
